\section{Analysis}
\label{sec:analysis}

\subsection{The router learns genre-conditional specialisation}
\label{subsec:routing-specialisation}

For each of six text genres (news, fiction, code, dialogue, technical exposition, urgent / emergency text) and each trained variant, we record the router's mean softmax distribution over all token positions in a representative passage. Results for the upper attention layer (the engaged one in all variants) are in Figure~\ref{fig:routing}; the lower layer follows the same pattern in muted form. We summarise specialisation strength via the maximum pairwise $L_1$ distance between the six genre distributions ($0$ = uniform, $2$ = disjoint).

(Averaging across both attention layers gives lower values, e.g.\ $0.60$ for \textsc{HR}, because the lower-layer routing is nearly uniform when the lower gate is closed --- see Section~\ref{sec:analysis}.) \textsc{HR}'s pattern is interpretable (news $\to$ urgency, fiction $\to$ curiosity, code $\to$ skepticism, urgent text $\to$ warmth) because its directions were extracted with semantic labels; the other variants' assignments are arbitrary because their banks contain unlabelled or random vectors. The structural similarity of specialisation across these very different banks is the load-bearing observation: the architecture+data system produces routing organisation regardless of bank content. Critically, the variant with the \emph{highest} specialisation strength (\textsc{HR-fixedgate}) achieves the same val PPL as the variant with the \emph{lowest} (\textsc{HR}) and as Base. \textsc{HR-noext}, despite the worst validation perplexity, also develops strong specialisation (layer-$11$ $L_1 \approx 1.4$; averaged across both attention layers $L_1 = 0.73$): its loss damage cannot be attributed to a failure to specialise. Across all five variants the specialisation pattern is structurally consistent --- only the labels (carried by the directions in \textsc{HR}) make some of these patterns interpretable to the reader; the routing geometry itself is universal.

\paragraph{Layer-asymmetric engagement.} The output gate $g$ at the upper attention layer (index $11$) opens during Stage-2 in every variant: $\textsc{HR}: +0.231$; $\textsc{HR-rand}: -0.122$; $\textsc{HR-fixedgate}: 1.0$ (frozen); $\textsc{HR-noext}: +0.366$; $\textsc{HR-forced}: +0.166$ (drifted down from $0.2$ initial). The lower attention layer (index $5$) stays effectively closed in the unforced variants ($|g| \leq 0.012$). Per-hormone learned magnitudes in \textsc{HR} grew modestly at the upper layer (range $1.06$--$1.14$) while remaining at initialisation at the lower layer ($0.97$--$0.98$), consistent with the asymmetric gate engagement: lower-layer magnitudes received no gradient signal because their contribution was masked by the closed gate. A working hypothesis, consistent with residual-stream organisation observed in transformers~\citep{zou2023representation}, is that the upper attention layer carries more decision-relevant signal and is therefore where the router's perturbation has somewhere useful to go. Our two attention layers are not sufficient to test this directly; the layer-asymmetric pattern is compatible with several alternative explanations (e.g., gradient-magnitude asymmetries independent of decision-relevance) and we do not attempt to distinguish them here.

\subsection{Discussion: organisation independent of objective}

The empirical pattern --- specialisation that develops across all bank choices, gate engagement non-trivial but small, validation perplexity invariant to all of this --- supports a non-trivial claim. \textbf{Specialisation strength does not correlate with downstream perplexity at this scale.} The variant with the highest specialisation (\textsc{HR-fixedgate}, $L_1 = 1.56$) achieves identical val PPL with the lowest-$L_1$ \textsc{HR} and with Base; only \textsc{HR-noext} changes the loss, and in the wrong direction. Two complementary readings are admissible: a \emph{structural} one, in which sufficient gradient reaches the router to produce a discriminative distribution but not enough reaches the gate (after the perturbation has been absorbed downstream) to push it into a regime that reshapes predictions; and a \emph{capacity-bottlenecked} one, in which $\dmodel = 1024$ is simply too low-dimensional for a small additive perturbation along seven fixed directions to reliably encode loss-reducing information.

The forced-engagement variant bears on both readings, but does not decide between them. \textsc{HR-forced} sits at an effective injection scalar $\mu \approx 0.5$, roughly $2\times$ the unforced variants; the gates drift \emph{downward} during training and per-hormone magnitudes shrink slightly --- the model uses every parameter it can modulate to reduce the effective magnitude. Because $\alpha$ is non-trainable it cannot fully escape, yet the residual perturbation it actually introduces stays under $0.2\%$ of the residual norm (measured directly below), and this does not damage the loss: validation loss at the matched step ($3.43$) is essentially identical to \textsc{HR}'s ($3.45$). We are explicit about the limit of this evidence: because the model keeps the effective injection so small, a loss-invariant outcome is exactly what the capacity-bottlenecked reading predicts, so this experiment does \emph{not} rule that reading out. What it does show is that the invariance survives an explicit attempt to enlarge the injection, and that the model's response is to shrink the channel rather than to exploit it. We use \emph{homeostatic envelope} descriptively, for the range of effective injection magnitudes over which we detect no change in validation perplexity --- not as an identified absorption mechanism.

\paragraph{Homeostatic envelope (informal definition).}
For a routing channel with output gate $g$, per-hormone magnitudes $m \in \mathbb{R}^{\nhormones}$, and direction bank $V \in \mathbb{R}^{\nhormones \times \dmodel}$, let the \emph{effective injection magnitude} at a residual position be the scalar $\mu = |g| \cdot \|m\|_1 / \nhormones$ (approximately $|g \cdot \bar m|$ with unit-norm $V$-rows and convex-combination routing). The \emph{homeostatic envelope} $\mathcal{E}$ of a trained model is the set of configurations $(g, m, V)$ compatible with the model's Stage-1-conditioned initialisation for which the resulting validation perplexity differs from the unmodified backbone by less than our detection threshold. We stress that this threshold is \emph{not} a training-noise floor: we did not measure independent-seed variance, so we cannot state how much of a validation-loss gap would survive across seeds. What we can state is that the four properly-initialised variants, all continued from the same Stage-1 checkpoint, track one another to within $<10^{-3}$ in loss throughout Stage 2; we use that lockstep-divergence bound as the operational threshold below which we report ``no detected difference,'' while noting it reflects the agreement of correlated continuations rather than a seed-level noise estimate. The definition is descriptive of the region we have empirically probed, not a fully-identified boundary: we test the model at a small number of $\mu$ values, we do not measure the actual residual perturbation $\|\Delta h\|_2 / \|h\|_2$ (which depends on per-token router weights, per-hormone magnitudes, and residual-stream scale), and we do not know the envelope's true boundary. What we can say empirically is that the envelope contains at least the range $\mu \in [0, 1.0]$ (the upper end set by \textsc{HR-fixedgate} at $\mu = 1$), is robust to arbitrary $V$ (extracted, random unit vectors, random-init-extracted introduced as a continuation), and that within this range the model expresses a preference for smaller $\mu$.

\noindent This is a descriptive label for the regime we observe --- not a separately-postulated mechanism. Figure~\ref{fig:envelope} plots each variant at its converged $(\mu, \text{val PPL})$ position; every properly-initialised variant sits on the horizontal $\mu$-axis band around \textsc{Base}'s PPL. The tested range spans $\mu \in [0, 1.0]$ (up to \textsc{HR-fixedgate}, whose gate is frozen at $1$); the unforced variants converge to $\mu \approx 0.1$--$0.25$. We do not identify the envelope's boundary --- doing so would require probing multiple $\mu$ values with independent runs and measuring the actual residual perturbation magnitude, which we leave to future work. The envelope can be broken on a different axis: introducing the routing during base pretraining (\textsc{HR-noext}) damages the loss even though the routing module still develops similar specialisation. The isolation ablation in Section~\ref{subsec:noext-cont} runs the same random-init-extracted bank as a staged continuation; comparing the observed conditions, the schedule delta ($+1.64$ PPL) is substantially larger than the bank delta ($+0.43$ PPL), but we do not claim a fully identified causal decomposition --- a from-scratch-with-extracted-good-bank arm would be required to close the crossed design.

\paragraph{What would falsify this picture.} A regime in which a forced perturbation \emph{does} change the validation loss --- found by larger $\alpha$, larger $\dmodel$, or longer training --- would identify the envelope's boundary on the magnitude axis. A hormone-routing variant whose specialisation pattern differs qualitatively from the others (e.g.\ no specialisation at all, or non-genre-discriminative) would weaken the structural reading. Neither condition was met by any variant in this study.

\paragraph{Controls for the specialisation claim.} A single-passage-per-genre measurement with a token-level permutation null (the analysis we presented in our initial submission) is vulnerable to two objections: (i) the observed $L_1$ could be an artefact of our particular passage choice, and (ii) tokens from the same document are correlated, so treating them as independent samples inflates the observed statistic relative to the null. We therefore also ran two stronger controls: a multi-document, document-level analysis and an untrained-router baseline. We additionally report the specialisation \emph{per variant and per layer} rather than for \textsc{HR-forced} at the engaged layer alone (Table~\ref{tab:per-layer}).

\emph{Multi-document analysis.} For each of the six genres we drew $20$ held-out documents (news from CNN/DailyMail, fiction from TinyStories, code from The Stack, dialog from PersonaChat, tech from arXiv summarisation, urgent from CNN/DailyMail filtered for emergency keywords), computed the per-document mean routing distribution at layer $11$, and computed the max pairwise $L_1$ across the six per-genre means-of-means. On the \textsc{HR-forced} checkpoint the observed $L_1 = 1.16$, essentially identical to the single-passage number ($1.17$); the specialisation is not driven by our particular passage choice.

\emph{Document-level permutation null.} Over $N=2000$ permutations that shuffle the document $\to$ genre assignment (documents are now the independent unit, not tokens), the null median is $L_1 = 0.30$, the 95th percentile is $0.41$, and the 99th percentile is $0.47$. The observed value $1.16$ is $\approx 2.5\times$ the null 99\%ile; empirical $p = 0/2000$. Compared to the token-level null we originally reported (median $0.41$, 99\%ile $0.62$), the document-level null is meaningfully tighter --- as expected, since documents are less correlated than adjacent tokens --- and the observed $L_1$ remains well above it.

\emph{Untrained-router baseline.} We ran the identical multi-document analysis on a freshly-initialised (untrained) \textsc{Unnatam} model with the same architecture. Because the underlying hybrid transformer's hidden states are already input-dependent, even a random projection produces some genre-conditional routing variation: the untrained observed $L_1 = 0.076$, which is itself above the untrained model's own document-level permutation null 99\%ile ($0.046$; empirical $p = 0$). But this baseline signal is $\approx 15\times$ smaller than the trained \textsc{HR-forced} model's ($1.16$ vs $0.076$); training amplifies the input-driven baseline by more than an order of magnitude. We interpret this as: the router's specialisation is not purely a projection of input-side differences (that projection is $\approx 0.08$), it is a genuinely learned amplification of them.

\emph{Effective residual perturbation magnitude.} $\mu$ as we defined it is a scalar coefficient on unit-norm bank vectors, not the actual perturbation on the residual stream. We now measure the latter directly by capturing $\|\Delta h\|_2$ and $\|h\|_2$ at every attention layer for every held-out document. For \textsc{HR-forced} the mean $\|\Delta h\|_2 / \|h\|_2$ is $0.15\%$ at the lower attention layer (layer $5$) and $0.07\%$ at the upper (layer $11$). Even under a $2\times$-amplified forced-engagement regime the routing channel's contribution is well under $1\%$ of the residual norm --- a concrete quantification of what the ``bounded homeostatic envelope'' means empirically. Because the permutation null depends on the checkpoint's router-output geometry, the same numerical bound is not automatically transferable across variants (we compute it on the \textsc{HR-forced} checkpoint and note its variant-dependence rather than claim a universal bound). The observed layer-$11$ $L_1$ values of $0.96$--$1.56$ across the other variants (Figure~\ref{fig:routing}) are all substantially above both null bounds we report.

\emph{Per-variant, per-layer specialisation.} We report the specialisation at \emph{both} hormone-bearing attention layers (indices $5$ and $11$) for every variant (Table~\ref{tab:per-layer}), rather than only the engaged layer of \textsc{HR-forced}. For \textsc{HR} and \textsc{HR-noext} the original inference captures recorded the router distribution both averaged over the two attention layers ($\bar l$) and at layer $11$ alone; since there are exactly two hormone-bearing layers, the layer-$5$ distribution is their exact difference, $l_5 = 2\bar l - l_{11}$ (each reconstructed genre row is a valid probability distribution: non-negative, summing to $1$). For the two checkpoints we retain (\textsc{HR-forced}, \textsc{HR-noext-cont}) we additionally run the full multi-document, document-level-null control at each layer. The pattern is consistent across every variant and both measurement modes: at the engaged upper layer the router specialises strongly ($L_1 = 0.96$--$1.56$), while at the lower layer specialisation is weak and near-uniform ($L_1 \approx 0.32$) in every variant whose lower gate is closed. Both retained checkpoints confirm this at the document-null level: at layer $11$ the observed multi-document $L_1$ is far above the document-level permutation-null 99th percentile ($\textsc{HR-forced}: 1.16$ vs.\ $0.47$; $\textsc{HR-noext-cont}: 0.90$ vs.\ $0.41$; both $p = 0$), while at layer $5$ the signal is much smaller though still above its (tighter) null ($\textsc{HR-forced}: 0.21$ vs.\ $0.095$; $\textsc{HR-noext-cont}: 0.20$ vs.\ $0.086$; both $p = 0$). \textsc{HR-forced}'s residual $\|\Delta h\|_2/\|h\|_2$ is $0.15\%$/$0.07\%$ at layers $5$/$11$; \textsc{HR-noext-cont}'s, with a closed lower gate, is $0.001\%$/$0.07\%$. The one exception we cannot measure is \textsc{HR-fixedgate}, whose lower gate is frozen \emph{open} ($g_5 = 1.0$): we expect its layer-$5$ routing to be specialised as well, but its converged checkpoint was not retained, so we leave that single cell unreported (a same-configuration re-run would fill it). The operative point is that specialisation strength tracks gate engagement at each layer --- which is the per-layer content of the claim.

\paragraph{Inference-time routing interventions.} The most direct test of whether the model \emph{uses} the routing structure is to intervene on it at inference and measure how far the predictions move. On a trained checkpoint we replace the learned per-token routing with a content-free alternative --- \emph{uniform} weights ($1/\nhormones$ at every position), \emph{shuffled} weights (each position's weights randomly permuted across the sequence), or a \emph{zero gate} (the routing channel removed entirely) --- and report, on the held-out corpus, the change in perplexity, the mean per-token KL divergence between the real-routing and intervened next-token predictive distributions, and the fraction of positions whose argmax prediction flips (\texttt{scripts/routing\_intervention\_kl.py}). On \textsc{HR-forced}, the largest-injection trained variant, all three interventions move the predictions negligibly: uniform routing gives mean $\mathrm{KL} = 4.7\times10^{-6}$ nats and a $0.12\%$ flip rate ($\Delta\mathrm{PPL} = +0.003$); shuffled gives $7.6\times10^{-6}$ nats and $0.16\%$ ($\Delta\mathrm{PPL} = +0.003$); and removing the channel entirely (zero gate) gives $1.7\times10^{-5}$ nats and $0.28\%$ ($\Delta\mathrm{PPL} = +0.0001$). Even fully disabling the routing changes the next-token distribution by $\approx 10^{-5}$ nats and flips the argmax on under $0.3\%$ of positions. This is direct evidence that, under the tested conditions, the model's predictions barely depend on the routing structure --- consistent with the reading that the absence of a detectable loss difference largely reflects how little the channel contributes, rather than an actively-absorbed large injection. Because the unforced variants drive their gates below \textsc{HR-forced}'s, their predictions depend on the routing even less, so these values bound the family; the second retained checkpoint, \textsc{HR-noext-cont} (a normal-gate variant), confirms this with an even smaller effect (uniform/shuffled/zero-gate all give $\mathrm{KL} \le 6.5\times10^{-7}$ nats and flip rates $\le 0.07\%$).

\paragraph{Behavioural steering probe.} The name ``hormone'' suggests a behavioural axis. Our trained 125M routing does not itself produce observable behavioural shifts in short generation samples --- the effective injection sits inside the homeostatic envelope and short prose is dominated by the base model's fluency. To test whether the extraction protocol nonetheless recovers behaviourally meaningful directions, we apply the same document-style contrast-pair extraction to a pretrained Mamba-1.4B model~\citep{gu2023mamba} and perform inference-time activation-addition steering following~\citet{turner2023activation, rimsky2024steering}: at each of the eight middle layers used for extraction, we add $\alpha \cdot v_i$ to the residual stream at every token position, then sample continuations of shared prompts. Appendix~\ref{app:steering} shows results across five prompts and all seven hormone directions at $\alpha \in \{0.5, 1.5\}$. The shifts are unambiguous and semantically aligned with the hormone labels. Selected examples: forced-ADR (urgency) at $\alpha = 1.5$ turns neutral prompts into alarm cascades (``\emph{'Aaaaaaaaaaaah!' The dog's teeth hit the door\ldots}'') and countdown sequences (``\emph{'Nine, eight, seven, six, five\ldots'}''); forced-NRA (persistence) reliably produces countdown structures with imperative repetition (``\emph{'RUN! RUN! RUN!'}''); forced-OXY (warmth) converts confrontational prompts into embracing intimate scenes (``\emph{she stroked its head. 'I'm so happy,' she said\ldots}''); forced-SRO (equanimity) produces contemplative, temporally-extended narratives; forced-SELF (self-coherence) produces first-person identity statements (``\emph{I am not a winner. I am not alone. I am not always right. I am not courageous.}''). We present these as illustrative demonstrations rather than a controlled result: we do not compare against matched-norm random directions and we score the shifts by inspection, so they are consistent with --- but do not by themselves establish --- the claim that the ``hormone'' naming labels an axis the extraction protocol identifies. Even taken at face value, they only concern the extraction protocol on Mamba-1.4B; the trained-routing regime at 125M does not deploy this axis at magnitudes large enough to reshape validation-set predictions.

\subsection{Limitations}

\paragraph{Scale and seeds.} Experiments are at $125$M parameters on a $2$B-token budget, single seed per variant. Scaling laws~\citep{hoffmann2022training} predict that the relative impact of architectural modifications can change with model size; the capacity-bottlenecked interpretation, which we do \emph{not} rule out at this scale (the model keeps the effective injection under $0.2\%$ of the residual norm, so a loss-invariant outcome is consistent with the channel simply contributing little), could equally govern larger $\dmodel$ or longer training. We also did not run independent seeds, so we cannot establish a training-noise floor: the lockstep convergence of the four properly-initialised variants ($<10^{-3}$ loss divergence) demonstrates that four continuations from a shared Stage-1 checkpoint agree closely, but because those continuations are correlated it is weaker evidence than independent-seed variance would be, and we do not treat it as a substitute. Establishing that a fraction of any cross-variant gap would persist across seeds would require the multi-seed runs we defer to future work.

\paragraph{HR-noext confounds three variables; the isolation ablation reduces but does not close the crossed design.} \textsc{HR-noext} differs from the other hormone variants on three dimensions: vectors extracted from a randomly-initialised model, training from scratch rather than continuation, and a $2$B contiguous budget rather than $1$B+$1$B staged. The \textsc{HR-noext-cont} ablation (Section~\ref{subsec:noext-cont}) runs the from-scratch bank as a staged continuation and lands at PPL $26.56$, giving us a schedule delta of $+1.64$ PPL (at fixed random-init bank) and a bank delta of $+0.43$ PPL (at fixed staged schedule). Both are descriptive comparisons between observed conditions, not a full causal decomposition: identifying the pure schedule effect would additionally require a from-scratch training with an extracted, benign bank — a fourth cell in the $2 \times 2$ schedule-by-bank design that we do not run. We report the descriptive split (larger schedule delta, smaller bank delta) and leave the crossed identification to future work. The qualitative reading we do commit to is that both axes contribute to the damage and neither is fully neutral in isolation.

\paragraph{Loss-decoupling is on validation PPL.} We did not measure effects on calibration, robustness, generation quality, or downstream fine-tuning behaviour --- any of which could in principle differ across variants while validation perplexity remains identical. We claim only that the routing organisation is decoupled from the validation perplexity objective the mechanism is trained against, not that it is decoupled from all downstream behaviour.

\paragraph{Pretraining-only and single-architecture; reference-scan throughput.} We do not perform supervised fine-tuning or RLHF, and both mechanisms are designed for the Mamba/MQA hybrid family. Hormone routing is architecture-agnostic in principle; intracellular attention generalises to any SSM admitting the slot interpretation. Neither is empirically validated outside our setting. Full-budget IA training requires a fused CUDA kernel.
