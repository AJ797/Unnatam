\begin{abstract}
We construct a controlled setting in which an architectural channel in a hybrid state-space/attention language model is free to organise its internal representations without measurably affecting the optimisation objective. The channel is \emph{hormone routing}: a token-conditional softmax-gated linear combination of a small bank of frozen direction vectors, added to the residual stream after each attention block. We pretrain a 125M-parameter Mamba/MQA hybrid on FineWeb-Edu under a fixed 2B-token budget across five variants of this channel --- extracted directions, random unit vectors, vectors extracted from a randomly-initialised model, and a frozen gate at $1.0$. The router develops semantically-coherent per-genre routing specialisation in every variant, with maximum pairwise $L_1$ distance between genre routing distributions (at the engaged upper attention layer) in $0.96$--$1.56$. Controls on the \textsc{HR-forced} checkpoint over $20$ held-out documents per genre confirm this: the observed $L_1 = 1.16$ is $\approx 2.5\times$ the document-level permutation-null 99th percentile ($0.47$) and $\approx 15\times$ larger than an untrained-router baseline ($0.076$), so the specialisation is not an artefact of passage selection nor a projection of input-side differences alone (Section~\ref{sec:analysis}). Crucially, the actual residual perturbation this channel introduces is very small: $\|\Delta h\|_2 / \|h\|_2 < 0.2\%$ across attention layers, even under $2\times$-amplified forced engagement. \textbf{Under the tested conditions we detect no difference in validation loss} between these variants: the four properly-initialised variants converge to identical validation perplexity ($26.13$) and their loss trajectories trace the same curve to within $10^{-3}$ throughout Stage 2. To probe whether this reflects an actively-absorbed injection or simply a negligible one, a sixth variant adds a forced non-zero gate initialisation and a non-trainable $3\times$ amplification. The model still drifts the gates downward and shrinks the per-hormone magnitudes, so the residual perturbation stays under $0.2\%$ of the residual norm; validation loss is again undamaged. We emphasise that this does \emph{not} establish that the channel is functionally irrelevant, that its output leaves predictions unchanged, or that a capacity bottleneck is excluded: because the model drives the effective injection so small, the absence of a detectable loss difference may largely reflect how little the channel ultimately contributes. We use the term \emph{homeostatic envelope} descriptively, for the range of effective injection magnitudes we have empirically probed without measuring a loss difference --- not as an identified mechanism or a boundary we have localised. Separately, the axis along which we \emph{do} observe measurable loss damage is the training schedule (introducing routing during base pretraining rather than as a continuation raises validation perplexity by $+2.07$ PPL). An isolation ablation reported in Section~\ref{subsec:noext-cont} runs the same random-init-extracted bank as a staged continuation and finds it recovers most of the way to Base: the observed damage splits between the two tested conditions as $\approx 1.64$ PPL from the schedule change (the from-scratch-vs-continuation delta at fixed bank) and $\approx 0.43$ PPL from the bank change (the extracted-vs-random-init-extracted delta at fixed staged schedule). This is a descriptive comparison between two observed conditions rather than a fully identified crossed causal decomposition (a from-scratch-with-extracted-good-bank arm would be required for the latter; we leave it to future work, Section~\ref{sec:analysis}). All results are at a single scale ($125$M parameters, $2$B tokens, one architecture family, single seed per variant); we hedge accordingly. Appendix~\ref{app:ia} sketches a second architectural mechanism --- intracellular attention, slot attention inside the SSM scan --- as future work.
\end{abstract}
